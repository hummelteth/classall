# Hack4Edu: a shared question becomes a support opportunity

Challenge 05, “Detectar antes de que sea demasiado tarde.” ClassAll recognizes support needs through voluntary reflection, explicit requests and limited Learning Check evidence, then enables human-led support. It does not identify individual academic risk or use machine learning.

Allow 6–8 minutes. Run the API and Vite using README instructions. Everything is fictional. Do not enter real student information. If dates have expired, use the additive example command in the README; do not delete the database. Use three different future days for group/tutor/faculty events to avoid schedule conflicts. No manual database changes are required.

1. **Recognize a need — Laura.** Open Learning Pulse → Mathematics → Chain rule. Choose “I'm struggling and would like support” and save. Show that she can update/withdraw. Request help; the explicit request is separate from her reflection. Reload to show persistence.
2. **Find community.** Follow the topic suggestion to study groups. Join the calculus group. The place count decreases and her home shows an upcoming circle. Open My groups, then leave; its place is restored. Optionally create her own circle and show edit/cancel.
3. **Offer support — David.** Switch to Tutor. Create a session in approved Mathematics / Chain rule. Use a unique title, meaningful objectives, a future date/time, 60 minutes, 6 places and a library location. Publish. It appears in My teaching and the common catalog. David can also use Find support and Pulse for his own learning. His original contribution points remain 40.
4. **Learn and reflect — Laura.** Switch back, find David’s new title and book. Complete the pre-check. My progress offers the labeled completed-session simulation; it changes neither attendance nor tutor points. Complete the post-check and review saved explanations. Refresh. The comparison describes answers to two short checks, not a causal intervention effect.
5. **Understand evidence — Professor García.** Teaching dashboard shows only assigned courses, current capacity and action summary. Open Learning insights. Three seeded synthetic Pulse participants and three seeded requests illustrate aggregate demand. Laura's contributions may alter these counts; group/booking matches change her request status. Two original seeded chain-rule check participants plus Laura can reach the threshold if she books the original chain-rule offering. When counts are smaller, results remain suppressed. Explain that Pulse, request and check evidence mean different things.
6. **Create an opportunity.** Select Plan a support action for Chain rule. Choose TA-led support, write an evidence-based rationale, optional follow-up, and check the human-review confirmation. Save. Expand the timestamped aggregate snapshot and its uncertainty.
7. **Organize synthetic faculty support.** Choose Organize synthetic TA session. Select approved synthetic host David, provide title/objectives and another future day. Confirm the offering. Professor García remains its organizer; David remains host. This is not an official university session.
8. **Close the cycle.** Switch to David to see the faculty offering in his teaching schedule. Switch to Laura and book it from the shared catalog. Show current capacity, professor’s linked action, learner reservation and persistence on refresh. The original booking system handles every reservation.
9. **Follow up.** Professor can update action status/notes/date. Demonstrate explicit cancellation if useful: confirmed reservations are cancelled visibly and saved checks remain. No automatic student contact or academic decision occurs.

Expected questions: identity is demo-only; n=3 suppression needs production redesign; thresholds are illustrative deterministic rules; there are no real grades, validated academic-risk predictions, institutional partnerships or measured educational outcomes. A low-resource pilot needs local Python/Node infrastructure, verified identities, reviewed content, tutor governance and a data-protection plan.
